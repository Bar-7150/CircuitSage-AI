## 1. Product Overview

**CircuitSage AI** is an AI-powered electronics debugging assistant designed to help students, hobbyists, makers, and junior engineers systematically troubleshoot malfunctioning breadboard circuits, microcontroller projects, and sensor integrations.

The application pairs local open-weight language and reasoning capabilities (**Google Gemma 4**, running via a compatible local inference runtime) with a **deterministic engineering rules engine**, an embedded **curated electronics knowledge base**, and persistent session tracking backed by **Express.js and Supabase Postgres**.

### Core Value Proposition
Unlike generic cloud chatbots that hallucinate pinouts, fail basic electrical arithmetic, or blur guesses with verified facts, CircuitSage AI enforces **epistemic honesty**:
* Electrical constraints (Ohm's law, maximum pin currents, logic level thresholds, and bus pull-ups) are computed deterministically.
* Unmeasured or unverified parameters remain explicitly tagged as `UNKNOWN`.
* Inferences generated by Gemma 4 are presented as tentative hypotheses accompanied by actionable multimeter verification tests.
* The system protects user privacy by defaulting to local AI processing without transmitting circuit photographs or measurements to third-party model providers.

## 2. Problem Statement

Electronics beginners face steep, multi-variable challenges when debugging physical hardware:
1. **Silent Failure Modes:** Unlike software programs that yield stack traces or error logs, physical circuits fail silently. An LED remains dark, an I2C display fails to initialize, or a microcontroller enters a brownout boot-loop without any diagnostic code.
2. **Information Fragmentation:** Resolving a fault typically requires cross-referencing multiple 50-page datasheets, pinout diagrams, forum threads, and application notes. Novices often miss critical constraints—such as 3.3V logic limits or required pull-up resistors.
3. **Destructive Consequences:** Incorrect assumptions about voltage, polarity, or current limitations can permanently destroy microcontrollers, burn sensors, or cause dangerous short circuits.
4. **Tool Uncertainty:** Beginners possessing a basic digital multimeter (DMM) often do not know where to place probes, which DMM mode to select, or how to interpret measured voltage drops.

## 3. Evidence Status: Known Facts vs. Assumptions & Hypotheses

To maintain engineering integrity, we explicitly distinguish validated facts from unverified assumptions and working hypotheses.

### 3.1 Known Facts (Empirically & Technically Grounded)
* **Fact 1:** Microcontroller GPIO pins have strict electrical ratings documented in manufacturer datasheets (e.g., ESP32 pin maximum current is 40 mA with 12 mA recommended; ESP32 GPIOs are not 5V tolerant; ATmega328P operates at 5V with 40 mA absolute max per pin).
* **Fact 2:** Open-weight LLMs run on consumer hardware via dedicated local runtimes (e.g., llama.cpp, Ollama, Transformers local servers), keeping user data strictly local.
* **Fact 3:** Pure language models are prone to mathematical calculation errors and electrical hallucinations when predicting resistance or pin ratings without external grounding.
* **Fact 4:** Supabase Postgres and Supabase Auth require an active network connection unless a local Supabase CLI emulator (Docker-based) is running on the host machine.

### 3.2 Assumptions Requiring Validation
* **Assumption A1 (Local Multimodal Availability):** A local Gemma 4 runtime on consumer hardware can reliably process uploaded circuit photos. *Status: Unconfirmed.* If image reasoning is unavailable or resource-prohibitive on target hardware, the MVP relies on structured text inputs and dropdown pin selections.
* **Assumption A2 (User Multimeter Fluency):** Target users can follow guided probe instructions (e.g., "Place RED probe on GPIO 18, BLACK probe on GND") using a standard digital multimeter.
* **Assumption A3 (Catalog Sufficiency):** A curated catalog of 15–20 common educational components covers $\ge 60\%$ of introductory laboratory and hobbyist troubleshooting scenarios.

### 3.3 Problem Hypotheses (To Be Validated Through Research)
* **Hypothesis H1:** Beginners will spend at least 40% less time debugging common breadboard errors when guided by a systematic multimeter probing loop compared to searching forums.
* **Hypothesis H2:** Users value explicit "UNKNOWN" badges and transparent datasheet citations over confident, ungrounded conversational answers.

## 4. Specific Target Users & Primary Use Case

### 4.1 Target User Cohorts
1. **Electronics & Engineering Students:** Undergraduates in electrical, computer, or mechatronics engineering taking introductory circuit and microcontroller lab courses.
2. **Arduino & ESP32 Beginners:** Self-directed learners building their first Internet of Things (IoT) or robotics projects.
3. **DIY Hobbyists & Makers:** Enthusiasts building custom hardware who own basic tools (breadboard, multimeter) but lack oscilloscopes or formal circuit theory training.

### 4.2 Primary Use Case
* **Scenario:** An engineering student connects an SSD1306 I2C OLED display and a DHT11 sensor to an ESP32 on a solderless breadboard. The code uploads successfully, but the OLED screen remains completely blank and the serial monitor displays `Failed to initialize I2C device`.
* **Action:** The student opens CircuitSage AI, selects "ESP32 DevKit v1", enters the symptom and pin configuration, and optionally uploads a photo of the breadboard.
* **Outcome:** CircuitSage AI queries its local knowledge base, deterministically detects missing pull-up resistors on the I2C bus lines, prompts the student to measure DC voltage on SDA/SCL, and guides them to add $4.7\text{k}\Omega$ resistors or enable internal pull-ups, successfully resolving the fault.

## 7. MVP Scope

The Minimum Viable Product (MVP) focuses on a reliable, demonstrable troubleshooting workflow for common breadboard microcontroller setups.

### 7.1 In-Scope Components (Curated Knowledge Base)
* **Microcontrollers & Dev Boards:** ESP32 DevKit v1 (3.3V logic), Arduino Uno R3 (ATmega328P, 5V logic), Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040, 3.3V logic).
* **Passives & Discretes:** Standard 5mm LEDs (Red, Green, Blue, White), carbon film resistors (E12 series), tactile momentary push-buttons, 10k potentiometers, 2N2222 NPN BJT, 2N7000 N-channel MOSFET, 1N400x rectifier diodes.
* **Sensors & Actuators:** SSD1306 0.96" I2C OLED display, DHT11 temperature/humidity sensor, 5V active buzzer.

### 7.2 In-Scope Fault Scenarios
1. **LED Series Resistor Fault:** Missing current-limiting resistor, undersized resistor causing overcurrent ($I > I_{\max}$), oversized resistor preventing illumination, or reversed polarity.
2. **Floating Digital Input:** Push-button connected to GPIO without an internal or external pull-up or pull-down resistor.
3. **Logic Level Voltage Incompatibility:** Direct connection of 5V sensor/output to a 3.3V microcontroller pin without level shifting.
4. **I2C Communication Breakdown:** Swapped SDA/SCL lines, missing bus pull-up resistors ($2.2\text{k}\Omega - 10\text{k}\Omega$), or address mismatch.
5. **Power Bus Discontinuity:** Breadboard split power rail disconnect or open ground return.

### 7.3 Core MVP Workflow Capabilities
* User authentication (via Supabase Auth) when online, with guest/local diagnostic session support.
* Structured problem intake: text description, target board selection, component list, and pin mappings.
* Optional circuit photo upload (stored via Supabase Storage when online, or local filesystem in offline mode).
* Local Gemma 4 inference generating ranked hypotheses and clarifying questions.
* Deterministic engineering rules engine evaluating electrical boundaries synchronously.
* Interactive measurement submission (voltage, resistance, continuity) dynamically updating diagnostic hypotheses.
* Clear epistemic badge classification: `VERIFIED_FACT`, `AI_INFERENCE`, `UNKNOWN`.
* Session history viewing and diagnostic report export (Markdown/JSON).

## 10. Functional Behavior of the AI Diagnosis Workflow

1. **Intake Normalization:** Request payload is validated by Express.js middleware using Joi or Zod schemas.
2. **Context Assembly:** Knowledge retrieval service fetches component specifications, pinout constraints, and known failure signatures from the local database.
3. **Deterministic Constraint Evaluation:** The rules engine evaluates known numerical parameters:
   * Current $I = (V_{supply} - V_f) / R$.
   * Logic compatibility: Checks if $V_{out,source} > V_{in,max,sink}$.
   * Bus pull-up: Checks if bus lines have pull-up resistors present.
4. **Local Gemma 4 Prompt Construction:** A structured system prompt injects retrieved datasheet constraints, deterministic results, and user symptoms, demanding a strict JSON response.
5. **Inference Execution:** Gemma 4 generates ranked hypotheses, human-readable explanations, and targeted test actions through the local inference adapter.
6. **State Persistence:** Case state, hypotheses, messages, and tests are saved to Supabase Postgres (when online) or local session storage (when offline).

## 12. Out-of-Scope Features (MVP Exclusions)

| Excluded Feature | Justification |
| :--- | :--- |
| **High-Voltage / AC Mains (>24V DC / >12V AC)** | **Lethal Safety Risk.** Electrocution and arc-flash hazards cannot be safely managed by an advisory assistant. |
| **Autonomous Hardware Actuation** | No automatic driving of bench power supplies or signal generators without direct human oversight. |
| **Arbitrary Code Execution** | Security boundary: The backend will not compile or execute arbitrary user/LLM firmware or scripts on the host OS. |
| **Full Numerical SPICE Simulation** | High computational overhead; requires complete SPICE netlists that beginners cannot supply. |
| **Automatic PCB Layout (Gerber/KiCad)** | Distinct engineering domain; detracts from the breadboard debugging focus. |
| **Training Foundation Models from Scratch** | Prohibitively expensive; Gemma 4 open weights provide excellent foundational reasoning via in-context retrieval. |

## 14. User Validation Plan (25-User Shogun Bar)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Provisional Milestone Plan:** The following protocol defines our operational plan to satisfy the **25-user bar at the Shogun milestone**. No interviews, usability trials, or user tests have taken place yet. All claims will be based solely on empirical data collected during Shogun execution.

### 14.1 Target Participant Qualification
To qualify, participants must meet at least one of the following profiles:
* Undergraduate engineering/computer science student currently enrolled in a hardware lab course.
* Active hobbyist who has built at least one Arduino, ESP32, or Raspberry Pi project in the past 6 months.
* Laboratory Teaching Assistant (TA) or electronics educator.

### 14.2 Recruitment Strategy
* 15 students recruited from university electronics and robotics clubs.
* 7 hobbyists recruited via regional maker communities and open-hardware meetups.
* 3 electronics lab teaching assistants.

### 14.3 Standardized Evaluation Tasks
Participants will be presented with one of three pre-staged physical breadboard circuits containing real-world faults:
* **Test Rig A (I2C Bus Fault):** ESP32 connected to an SSD1306 OLED display with omitted bus pull-up resistors.
* **Test Rig B (Floating Input Fault):** Arduino Uno connected to a momentary button with no pull-up/pull-down resistor.
* **Test Rig C (LED Series Fault):** ESP32 connected to an LED with reversed polarity and an undersized current-limiting resistor ($10\ \Omega$).

### 14.4 Data Collection & Measurement
* **Quantitative Data:** Time to successful fault identification; number of multimeter probe iterations; binary success/failure.
* **Qualitative Survey:** 5-question Likert survey assessing explanation clarity, trust in epistemic badges, and perceived safety.
* **Feedback Mechanism:** In-app feedback submission endpoint (`POST /api/v1/feedback`) logging rating, usability comments, and session ID.

### 14.5 Pass/Fail Criterion for Shogun Milestone
* **PASS:** At least 19 out of 25 participants ($\ge 76\%$) successfully diagnose and correct the circuit fault within 20 minutes, with average usefulness rating $\ge 4.0/5.0$ and zero safety failures.
* **FAIL:** Completion rate $< 70\%$, or any safety-critical error resulting in component destruction.

## 15. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk ID | Description | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RSK-01** | Local Gemma 4 inference latency is too high on low-end hardware. | High | Implement quantized 4-bit weights; provide text-first fallback when multimodal processing is slow. |
| **RSK-02** | User enters incorrect multimeter readings into the app. | Medium | Provide explicit multimeter dial-setting diagrams and probe sanity checks (e.g., flag unexpected negative voltages). |
| **RSK-03** | Cloud Supabase unavailable in offline lab environments. | High | Clearly document offline boundaries; implement local JSON/SQLite persistence fallback for air-gapped sessions. |
| **RSK-04** | LLM hallucination of pin voltages or pinout assignments. | Critical | Enforce deterministic rule override: rule engine verifies all pin assignments against local datasheet DB before output. |
